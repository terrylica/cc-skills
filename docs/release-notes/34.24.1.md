## Narrative

This release documents why a Braille Tally can turn into a grid of hollow rings, and how to stop it. In Chrome on macOS, a tally in bold text draws every cell as a full eight-ring grid, filled where a dot is raised and hollow where it is not, so the levels that make a tally readable at a glance disappear. GitHub's issue list is where most people will meet it, because list titles there are semibold, while the same title inside the issue is regular weight and looks right. The cause, measured on one test page across engines and weights: macOS ships five faces in the "Apple Braille" family, all registered at weight 400, and the Outline faces draw a ring at every unraised position by design. At regular weight the fallback lands on the Regular face; at 500 and above Chromium lands on an Outline face. Safari's engine and Chrome and Edge on Windows draw raised dots only at every weight. The skill now explains this, gives a CSS alias that fixes it for a page or a user stylesheet, and warns off the two fixes that look right but are not. A title's own text cannot carry the fix, and the plugin's drawn HTML form needs no font, so it was never affected.

## Changed

- **New skill section "Reading tallies on macOS Chrome"** (`plugins/braille-tally/skills/braille-tally/SKILL.md`): the symptom, the cause, and a measured table of three engines at weight 400 and at 500–700 (#135).
- **The fix:** `@font-face { font-family: "Braille Solid"; src: local("AppleBraille"); font-weight: 100 900; unicode-range: U+2800-28FF; }`, placed first in the font stack, tested solid in both macOS engines at 600 and harmless where the face is absent (#135).
- **Two warnings, both measured:** use the PostScript name, because `local("Apple Braille")` made WebKit pick an Outline face; and do not name `"Apple Braille"` in `font-family`, Chromium's 2023 workaround (issue 40275082, closed Won't Fix), which is now hollow in both engines at 400 and 600 (#135).
- **README Limits** gains a one-line summary pointing to the section, and the evolution log records the finding and its evidence (#135).

## Known limits

- The alias only helps where a page's CSS, a user stylesheet or an extension can apply it; on GitHub itself the list titles stay hollow in macOS Chrome unless the reader adds one.
- iPhone browsers were not measured.
